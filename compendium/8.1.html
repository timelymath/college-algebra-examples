8.1 The Ellipse

What makes an ellipse

Pick two fixed points, called the foci (one is a focus). An ellipse is every point whose distances to the two foci add up to the same total.

Pin a 10-inch string at the two foci, pull it tight with a pencil, and trace: you've drawn this ellipse. For every ellipse the constant total is called $2\ka{a}$, so here $\ka{a}=5$.

Why it's called a conic

Slice a cone with a tilted plane and the cut edge is an ellipse. Other slices give the hyperbola (8.2) and the parabola (8.3).

The parts, and the equation that links them

Center
The middle. Everything else is measured from here.
Vertices
Ends of the long major axis, $\ka{a}$ from the center.
Co-vertices
Ends of the short minor axis, $\kb{b}$ from the center.
Foci
Inside, on the major axis, $\kc{c}$ from the center.

Where $\kc{c}^2=\ka{a}^2-\kb{b}^2$ comes from

Stand at a co-vertex. The two foci are equally far away, and the two distances add to $2\ka{a}$, so each one is $\ka{a}$. That's the slanted side of a right triangle with legs $\kb{b}$ and $\kc{c}$. In the picture, $\kb{b}=3$, $\kc{c}=4$, and $\ka{a}=5$.

$$\kb{b}^2+\kc{c}^2=\ka{a}^2 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \kc{c}^2=\ka{a}^2-\kb{b}^2$$

So $a$ is always the largest of the three.

Optional: deriving the equation in five lines

Put the foci at $(\pm c, 0)$. For any point $(x, y)$ on the ellipse, the distances to the foci add to $2a$:

$$\begin{aligned} \sqrt{(x+c)^2+y^2}+\sqrt{(x-c)^2+y^2}&=2a &&\text{the definition}\\ a^2-cx&=a\sqrt{(x-c)^2+y^2} &&\text{isolate a root, square, simplify}\\ (a^2-c^2)\,x^2+a^2y^2&=a^2(a^2-c^2) &&\text{square again, simplify}\\ b^2x^2+a^2y^2&=a^2b^2 &&\text{let } b^2=a^2-c^2\\ \frac{x^2}{a^2}+\frac{y^2}{b^2}&=1 &&\text{divide by } a^2b^2 \end{aligned}$$

Standard form at a glance

One equation covers every ellipse in this section. The only question is which way it stretches.

Horizontal: wider than tall

$$\frac{(x-\kh{h})^2}{\ka{a}^2}+\frac{(y-\kh{k})^2}{\kb{b}^2}=1$$
  • Vertices$(\kh{h}\pm\ka{a},\ \kh{k})$
  • Co-vertices$(\kh{h},\ \kh{k}\pm\kb{b})$
  • Foci$(\kh{h}\pm\kc{c},\ \kh{k})$

Vertical: taller than wide

$$\frac{(x-\kh{h})^2}{\kb{b}^2}+\frac{(y-\kh{k})^2}{\ka{a}^2}=1$$
  • Vertices$(\kh{h},\ \kh{k}\pm\ka{a})$
  • Co-vertices$(\kh{h}\pm\kb{b},\ \kh{k})$
  • Foci$(\kh{h},\ \kh{k}\pm\kc{c})$

Both: $\ka{a}>\kb{b}$ and $\kc{c}^2=\ka{a}^2-\kb{b}^2$. Centered at the origin? Then $\kh{h}=\kh{k}=0$ and the form is just $\dfrac{x^2}{a^2}+\dfrac{y^2}{b^2}=1$ (or $a^2$ under $y$).

  1. Make the right side 1.If it isn't, divide every term by the constant.
  2. Flip the signs for the center.$(x+2)^2$ means $x-(-2)$, so $\kh{h}=-2$.
  3. The bigger denominator is $\ka{a}^2$.Its variable tells you the long direction.
A

Write the equation from vertices and foci

The routine

  1. Direction. Vertices share a $y$-value: horizontal. They share an $x$-value: vertical.
  2. Center. The midpoint of the vertices.
  3. $\ka{a}$ and $\kc{c}$. Center to a vertex is $\ka{a}$. Center to a focus is $\kc{c}$.
  4. $\kb{b}^2$. Solve $\kc{c}^2=\ka{a}^2-\kb{b}^2$, then substitute.

Given just one vertex and one focus? Symmetry gives you the other two.

Example 1

Writing the Equation of an Ellipse Centered at the Origin in Standard Form

What is the standard form equation of the ellipse that has vertices $(\pm8,\,0)$ and foci $(\pm5,\,0)$?

  1. Direction. The points sit on the $x$-axis, so the ellipse is horizontal and centered at $\kh{(0,0)}$: $$\frac{x^2}{\ka{a}^2}+\frac{y^2}{\kb{b}^2}=1$$
  2. Read $\ka{a}$ and $\kc{c}$. Vertices: $\ka{a}=8$, so $\ka{a}^2=64$. Foci: $\kc{c}=5$, so $\kc{c}^2=25$.
  3. Find $\kb{b}^2$. $$\begin{aligned}\kc{c}^2&=\ka{a}^2-\kb{b}^2\\ \kc{25}&=\ka{64}-\kb{b}^2\\ \kb{b}^2&=39\end{aligned}$$
  4. Answer$$\frac{x^2}{64}+\frac{y^2}{39}=1$$

Example 2

Writing the Equation of an Ellipse Centered at a Point Other Than the Origin

What is the standard form equation of the ellipse that has vertices $(-2,-8)$ and $(-2,2)$ and foci $(-2,-7)$ and $(-2,1)$?

  1. Direction. The $x$-values match, so the ellipse is vertical: $$\frac{(x-\kh{h})^2}{\kb{b}^2}+\frac{(y-\kh{k})^2}{\ka{a}^2}=1$$
  2. Center. The midpoint of the vertices: $$\begin{aligned}(\kh{h},\kh{k})&=\left(\frac{-2+(-2)}{2},\ \frac{-8+2}{2}\right)\\&=\kh{(-2,-3)}\end{aligned}$$
  3. Read $\ka{a}$ and $\kc{c}$. Center up to the vertex $(-2,2)$: $\ka{a}=2-(-3)=5$. Center up to the focus $(-2,1)$: $\kc{c}=1-(-3)=4$.
  4. Find $\kb{b}^2$. $\kc{16}=\ka{25}-\kb{b}^2$, so $\kb{b}^2=9$.
  5. Answer$$\frac{(x+2)^2}{9}+\frac{(y+3)^2}{25}=1$$

B

Graph an ellipse from its equation

The routine

  1. Standard form. Make the right side 1.
  2. Center. Read $(\kh{h},\kh{k})$ with the signs flipped.
  3. $\ka{a}$ and $\kb{b}$. The bigger denominator is $\ka{a}^2$. Take square roots.
  4. $\kc{c}$. From $\kc{c}^2=\ka{a}^2-\kb{b}^2$.
  5. Plot. From the center, step $\ka{a}$ the long way, $\kb{b}$ across, and $\kc{c}$ to each focus. Sketch through the four ends.
Example 3

Graphing an Ellipse Centered at the Origin

Graph $\dfrac{x^2}{9}+\dfrac{y^2}{25}=1$. Identify and label the center, vertices, co-vertices, and foci.

  1. Center. No shifts, so $\kh{(0,0)}$.
  2. Direction. $25>9$, and $\ka{a}^2=25$ sits under $y$: vertical. $\ka{a}=5$ and $\kb{b}=3$.
  3. Find $\kc{c}$. $$\begin{aligned}\kc{c}^2&=\ka{a}^2-\kb{b}^2\\&=25-9=16\\ \kc{c}&=4\end{aligned}$$
  4. Plot.
    • Vertices$(0,\,\pm5)$
    • Co-vertices$(\pm3,\,0)$
    • Foci$(0,\,\pm4)$

Example 4

Graphing an Ellipse Centered at the Origin from an Equation Not in Standard Form

Graph $4x^2+25y^2=100$. Rewrite the equation in standard form, then identify and label the center, vertices, co-vertices, and foci.

  1. Standard form. Divide every term by 100: $$\frac{4x^2}{100}+\frac{25y^2}{100}=\frac{100}{100}\ \Longrightarrow\ \frac{x^2}{25}+\frac{y^2}{4}=1$$
  2. Direction. $25>4$, under $x$: horizontal, centered at $\kh{(0,0)}$. $\ka{a}=5$ and $\kb{b}=2$.
  3. Find $\kc{c}$. $\kc{c}^2=25-4=21$, so $\kc{c}=\sqrt{21}\approx4.6$.
  4. Plot.
    • Vertices$(\pm5,\,0)$
    • Co-vertices$(0,\,\pm2)$
    • Foci$\left(\pm\sqrt{21},\,0\right)$

Example 5

Graphing an Ellipse Centered at (h, k)

Graph $\dfrac{(x+2)^2}{4}+\dfrac{(y-5)^2}{9}=1$. Identify and label the center, vertices, co-vertices, and foci.

  2. Direction. $9>4$, under $y$: vertical. $\ka{a}=3$ and $\kb{b}=2$.

Example 6

Graphing an Ellipse Centered at (h, k) by First Writing It in Standard Form

Graph $4x^2+9y^2-40x+36y+100=0$. Identify and label the center, vertices, co-vertices, and foci.

  1. Group, and move the constant. $$(4x^2-40x)+(9y^2+36y)=-100$$
  2. Factor out the squared terms' coefficients. $$4(x^2-10x)+9(y^2+4y)=-100$$
  3. Complete each square. Half of $-10$ is $-5$, and $(-5)^2=25$. Half of $4$ is $2$, and $2^2=4$. $$\begin{aligned}4(x^2-10x+\kn{25})&+9(y^2+4y+\kn{4})\\&=-100+\kn{4\cdot25}+\kn{9\cdot4}\end{aligned}$$
  4. Rewrite as squares. $$4(x-5)^2+9(y+2)^2=36$$
  5. Divide by 36. $$\frac{(x-5)^2}{9}+\frac{(y+2)^2}{4}=1$$
  6. Read and plot. Center $\kh{(5,-2)}$. $9>4$, under $x$: horizontal, with $\ka{a}=3$, $\kb{b}=2$, $\kc{c}=\sqrt{9-4}=\sqrt5$.
    • Vertices$(2,-2)$, $(8,-2)$
    • Co-vertices$(5,-4)$, $(5,0)$
    • Foci$\left(5\pm\sqrt5,\,-2\right)$

Why add 100 and not 25? The 25 lands inside $4(\;\;)$, so the left side really grew by $4\cdot25=100$. Add the same amount to the right side.

D

Solve applied problems

Planets orbit the sun with the sun at one focus. A lithotripter aims sound waves from one focus at a kidney stone sitting at the other. Whispering chambers use the same reflection you saw at the top of this section.

Example 7

Locating the Foci of a Whispering Chamber

A room in an art gallery is a whispering chamber, 96 feet long and 46 feet wide. (a) Write the standard form equation of its outline, with the center at $(0,0)$ and the long side horizontal. (b) Two visitors stand at the foci. How far apart are they, to the nearest foot?

  1. (a) Read $\ka{a}$ and $\kb{b}$. Length is the major axis: $2\ka{a}=96$, so $\ka{a}=48$ and $\ka{a}^2=2304$. Width is the minor axis: $2\kb{b}=46$, so $\kb{b}=23$ and $\kb{b}^2=529$.
    (a)$$\frac{x^2}{2304}+\frac{y^2}{529}=1$$
  2. (b) Find $\kc{c}$. $$\begin{aligned}\kc{c}^2&=\ka{a}^2-\kb{b}^2\\&=2304-529=1775\\ \kc{c}&=\sqrt{1775}\approx42\end{aligned}$$ The foci are at $(\pm42,0)$.
    (b)$$2\kc{c}\approx2(42)=84\text{ feet}$$

Try It#7

A whispering chamber is 480 feet long and 320 feet wide. (a) Write the standard form equation of the room, centered at $(0,0)$ with the long side horizontal. (b) Two people stand at the foci. How far apart are they, to the nearest foot?

Check your answer
(a) $\dfrac{x^2}{57{,}600}+\dfrac{y^2}{25{,}600}=1$, from $a=240$ and $b=160$. (b) $c^2=57{,}600-25{,}600=32{,}000$, so $c\approx178.9$ and the people stand about 358 feet apart.

Six mistakes to avoid

  • Using $c^2=a^2+b^2$That's the hyperbola in 8.2. In an ellipse the foci are inside, so $c$ is shorter than $a$: $c^2=a^2-b^2$.
  • Assuming $a^2$ goes under $x$$a^2$ is the bigger denominator, wherever it sits.
  • Forgetting to flip the sign$(y+3)^2$ means $k=-3$, not $3$.
  • Stepping out $a^2$ instead of $a$In $\dfrac{y^2}{25}$, the vertices are 5 units from the center, not 25.
  • Adding 25 instead of $4\cdot25$After factoring out 4, the amount added to the right side is $4\cdot25$.
  • Reading $a^2$ before the right side is 1In $4x^2+25y^2=100$, divide every term by 100 first. The denominators are 25 and 4, not 4 and 25.

Section 8.1 in four lines

  • An ellipse is every point whose distances to two foci add to $2a$.
  • Standard form: $\dfrac{(x-h)^2}{\Box}+\dfrac{(y-k)^2}{\Box}=1$. The bigger denominator is $a^2$ and points the long way.
  • $c^2=a^2-b^2$, so $a$ is always the largest.
  • From the center: vertices are $a$ away, co-vertices $b$, foci $c$.
